# One issuer after the argon2 fix, 20 November 2026

Every run since October has found one imbu issuer saturating at about 1.4 to 1.6 credentials a second, and three issuers at about 4. imbu-stamps' CPU profile explained why (`~/code/bakobo/imbu/.ignored/issuance-cpu-profile-2026-11.md`): 0.575 s of issuer CPU per credential, 0.49 s of it in argon2 `crypto_pwhash`, called once to blind the credential at issue and again to re-derive its disclosure at grant. Two heti changes followed. heti#74 lets a grant reuse the blinder heti kept at issue, so a credential pays argon2 once. heti#75 derives the issue-side blind on a process-wide pool of four threads, so concurrent issuances on one issuer stop queuing behind it on the event loop. imbu `7b544ac` (bakobo/imbu#49) pins the heti that carries both, `093acb3`.

This run asks how much that moved one issuer's capacity.

## The answer

One issuer at the new pin kept up with every rate offered, up to 4.0 a second, with no refusals and nothing lost. At the old pin it saturated at about 1.5 a second, as every earlier run had. The fix lifted one issuer's capacity at least 2.6 times, and the ceiling now lies somewhere above 4.0, which this run did not reach.

| offered a second | old pin: witnessed a second | old pin: p50 requested → witnessed | new pin: witnessed a second | new pin: p50 / p99 requested → witnessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 1.00 | 0.40 s | 1.00 | 0.40 s / 0.55 s |
| 1.5 | 1.48 | 0.67 s | 1.50 | 0.39 s / 0.47 s |
| 2.0 | 1.54 | 10.0 s | 2.00 | 0.39 s / 0.52 s |
| 2.8 | 1.59 | 27.0 s | 2.82 | 0.41 s / 0.53 s |
| 3.5 | 1.45 | 31.9 s | 3.52 | 0.42 s / 0.70 s |
| 4.0 | 1.51 | 36.5 s | 4.00 | 0.43 s / 0.54 s |

At the old pin, from 2.8 a second up, credentials were lost: 4 of 140 at 2.8, 31 of 175 at 3.5 and 36 of 200 at 4.0, each refused or timed out. At 4.0 its issuer answered the adapter's sends with 2,540 of the answer budget's 429s and 565 of its 503 `e.env.saturated.r`. At the new pin, at 4.0, it answered none of either, and all 200 credentials requested were witnessed, one of them just after the window closed.

## Where the time goes

imbu's two new stamps, `constructed` and `registry_updated`, split what used to be one accepted → anchored stage. Medians, in milliseconds:

| offered a second | pin | requested → accepted | accepted → constructed | constructed → registry_updated | registry_updated → anchored | anchored → witnessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | old | 5 | 15 | 250 | 16 | 104 |
| 1.0 | new | 5 | 15 | 248 | 17 | 108 |
| 2.8 | old | 495 | 21,525 | 2,366 | 1,697 | 156 |
| 2.8 | new | 7 | 16 | 243 | 17 | 118 |
| 4.0 | old | 12,969 | 22,330 | 1,690 | 936 | 466 |
| 4.0 | new | 6 | 15 | 266 | 17 | 112 |

Below saturation the two pins are the same, and a credential's latency is dominated by constructed → registry_updated, about 0.25 s. That is one argon2 pass, which both pins pay on the latency path at issue, plus heti's wait for an earlier act to reach toad (imbu-stamps' description, which this run does not separate). So the fix does not make a single credential faster, and was not expected to. What it changes is how many credentials an issuer can carry at once. At the old pin, once offered load passed about 1.5 a second, the queue built in front of construction: accepted → constructed rose from 15 ms to 22 s, which is imbu's bounded backlog of about 30 s of work, and the rest was turned away. At the new pin every stage stayed at its unloaded value through 4.0 a second.

## What this does not show

The run cannot say how much of the gain each heti change bought. Halving argon2 alone would, by arithmetic from the profile, have lifted the CPU ceiling from about 1.75 to about 3 a second. The issuer passed 4.0, so the thread pool, which lets argon2 for concurrent credentials run on several cores at once, is plausibly most of the rest. Separating them would take a run at a heti with heti#74 and not heti#75.

Each rate is one 60 s window, a probe. Keeping up for a minute at 4.0 is not keeping up for an hour, and the peak-hour scenario at one issuer, which failed at 2.8 a second on every earlier pin (`docs/targets-2026-10-rerun.md`), is the test of that. It is worth running next, together with a probe above 4.0 to find the new ceiling, and the three-issuer probe: if argon2 now spreads over four cores per issuer, three issuers on this eight-core host may contend for CPU in a way they did not before.

The old pin ran second. Each run's provenance records the host's one-minute load across its window: 1.9 to 4.7 at the new pin, rising with the offered rate, and 1.9 to 4.2 at the old pin, highest at its unsaturated rates and lowest, 1.9 to 3.1, at the rates where it saturated. So the old pin's lower capacity is not explained by a busier host: it saturated while the host was quieter than during the new pin's runs at the same rates. Some of that load is the stack under test; the run cannot say how much came from other work on the host. `loadavg.log` on each side covers only the scenario's four rates, because `scripts/targets.sh` stops its logger before the two added ones, which is why the per-run figures here come from provenance.

## How it was run

On kelun, on 20 November 2026, under the shared `~/.local/state/bench-host.lock`, starting once the one-minute load fell under 2 (it was 1.40). `probe-capacity-1home` ran at its four rates through `scripts/targets.sh`, then at 3.5 and 4.0 with `keri-bench run --rate`, first at the new pin, harness `81c2ca5`, imbu `7b544ac`, heti `093acb3`, and then at the old pin, harness `1d59ce2`, imbu `317ae49`, heti `12e6f41`. Each side calibrated first under the strict gate and passed. Why it was run this way is `this.i` 279zu9ps, under the pin move 06klkn9m.

The runs are in `results/argon2-2026-11`, `after/` for the new pin and `before/` for the old, with the driver script `run.sh` and its log `run.log`.
